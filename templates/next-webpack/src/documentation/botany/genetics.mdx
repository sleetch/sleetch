# Genetics & Breeding

The genetics of irises have been studied extensively, both for **scientific understanding** and **horticultural improvement**. With over 300 species and countless cultivars, irises exhibit remarkable genetic diversity.

## Chromosome Structure

### Chromosome Numbers

| **Subgenus** | **Chromosome Number (2n)** | **Genome Size** | **Ploidy** |
|--------------|----------------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Iris (Bearded) | 24, 32, 40, 48 | Large | 2x, 3x, 4x, 5x |
| Limniris (Beardless) | 24, 32, 36, 40, 48 | Medium | 2x, 3x, 4x |
| Xiphium (Bulbous) | 10, 20, 30, 40 | Small | 2x, 3x, 4x |
| Nevskia | 16, 32 | Small | 2x, 4x |
| Scorpiris | 10, 20 | Small | 2x |
| Hermodactyloides | 10, 20 | Small | 2x |

> **Note**: The **bearded irises** (Subgenus *Iris*) show the most **polyploidy**, with chromosome numbers ranging from diploid (2n=24) to pentaploid (2n=48).

### Karyotype Analysis

```
Typical Iris Karyotype (2n=24):
├── 12 pairs of chromosomes

```

## Genetic Inheritance

### Color Inheritance

Iris flower color is controlled by **multiple gene pairs** following **Mendelian inheritance** patterns:

#### Basic Color Genes

| **Gene** | **Alleles** | **Effect** | **Inheritance** |
|----------|-------------|------------|-----------------|
| **A** | A (dominant), a (recessive) | Anthocyanin production | Complete dominance |
| **B** | B (dominant), b (recessive) | Blue vs. purple | Incomplete dominance |
| **C** | C (dominant), c (recessive) | Color intensity | Additive |
| **I** | I (dominant), i (recessive) | Inhibitor (white) | Epistasis |

#### Color Inheritance Examples

```
Purple × White (AABBcc × aabbCC):
├── F1: AaBbCc (Purple)
└── F2: 9 Purple : 3 Blue : 4 White

Yellow × Purple (aaBBcc × AABBcc):
├── F1: AaBBcc (Purple)
└── F2: 3 Purple : 1 Yellow
```

### Pattern Inheritance

| **Pattern** | **Gene** | **Inheritance** | **Example** |
|-------------|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Self | s | Recessive | 'Blue Flag' |
| Bicolor | b | Dominant | 'Conjuration' |
| Plicata | p | Dominant | 'Honky Tonk Blues' |
| Variegata | v | Dominant | 'Variegata' |
| Amoena | a | Recessive | 'Amoena' |

## Molecular Genetics

### DNA Sequencing

```

├── rbcL
│   └── Chloroplast gene

### Gene Expression

The expression of **flower color genes** varies during development:

```
Gene Expression Timeline:
├── Stage 1: Bud Formation
│   ├── CHS (Chalcone Synthase) ↑
│   └── Color precursor production
├── Stage 2: Early Flower Development
│   ├── F3H (Flavanone 3-Hydroxylase) ↑
│   └── Anthocyanin pathway activation
├── Stage 3: Maturation
│   ├── DFR (Dihydroflavonol 4-Reductase) ↑
│   └── Final color development
└── Stage 4: Full Bloom
    ├── ANS (Anthocyanidin Synthase) ↑
    └── Color stabilization
```

## Breeding Techniques

### Traditional Breeding

#### Cross-Pollination

```md
**Steps for Manual Pollination**:

1. **Select Parents**
   - Choose plants with desired traits
   - Ensure compatibility (same subgenus)
   - Consider bloom time synchronization

2. **Emasculation** (Optional)
   - Remove anthers from seed parent
   - Prevents self-pollination
   - Use fine tweezers

3. **Pollination**
   - Collect pollen from father plant
   - Apply to stigma of mother plant
   - Best done in morning (9-11 AM)

4. **Labeling**
   - Tag pollinated flowers
   - Record parentage
   - Note date of pollination

5. **Seed Collection**
   - Wait for capsule to mature (6-8 weeks)
   - Harvest when capsule turns brown
   - Dry seeds before storage
```

#### Breeding Goals

| **Trait** | **Gene(s)** | **Breeding Method** | **Difficulty** |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| New Colors | A, B, C | Cross different color parents | Medium |
| Larger Blooms | Size genes | Select for largest flowers | Easy |
| Disease Resistance | R genes | Cross with resistant species | Hard |
| Extended Bloom | Flowering genes | Cross early and late bloomers | Medium |
| Fragrance | F genes | Cross fragrant species | Medium |
| Drought Tolerance | D genes | Cross with xeric species | Hard |

#### Genetic Engineering

While not yet widely used in iris breeding, **genetic engineering** techniques show promise:

```
Potential Applications:
├── CRISPR-Cas9
│   ├── Target: Color genes (A, B, C)
│   └── Result: Novel color combinations
├── Gene Silencing
│   ├── Target: Inhibitor genes (I)
│   └── Result: Enhanced color expression
├── Gene Transfer
│   ├── Source: Other plant species
│   └── Result: New traits (disease resistance, fragrance)
└── Marker-Assisted Selection
    ├── Use: DNA markers for trait selection
    └── Benefit: Faster breeding cycles
```

## Hybrid Registration

### Registration Process

To officially register a new iris cultivar:

1. **Grow and Evaluate**
   - Minimum 3 years of evaluation
   - Ensure stability and uniformity
   - Document characteristics

2. **Name Selection**
   - Unique name (not previously used)
   - Follows ICNCP rules
   - No offensive or misleading names

3. **Documentation**
   - Parentage information
   - Description of traits
   - Photographs
   - Growing requirements

4. **Registration**
   - Submit to American Iris Society
   - Pay registration fee
   - Receive official registration number

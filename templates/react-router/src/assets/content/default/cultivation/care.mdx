# Seasonal Care Guide

> **"Irises are the gift that keeps on giving - with the right care, they'll bloom for generations."**

Proper **seasonal care** is the key to **healthy, vibrant irises** that bloom year after year. This guide will walk you through what to do **each month** to keep your irises thriving.

## Annual Care Calendar

### 🌱 **Spring** (March - May)

#### Early Spring (March - Early April)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Remove winter mulch** gradually as soil warms
- [x] **Clean up** old foliage from previous year
- [x] **Check for heaving**: Press down any rhizomes exposed by frost
- [x] **Fertilize**: Apply low-nitrogen fertilizer (6-10-10)
- [x] **Divide** clumps if needed (best done in early spring or fall)
- [x] **Weed**: Remove early weeds before they spread
- [x] **Pest control**: Check for iris borer eggs

**What to Expect**:
- New growth emerges from rhizomes
- Flower buds begin to form
- Early varieties may start blooming
```

#### Mid Spring (April - Early May)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Water**: Provide 1 inch per week if rainfall is insufficient
- [x] **Deadhead**: Remove spent blooms to prevent seed formation
- [x] **Stake**: Support tall varieties if needed
- [x] **Mulch**: Apply 2-3 inches of mulch to retain moisture
- [x] **Pest control**: Monitor for aphids and thrips

**What to Expect**:
- **Peak bloom season** for most varieties
- Bearded irises bloom first, followed by Siberian and Japanese
- Rapid growth and foliage development
```

#### Late Spring (May - Early June)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Deadhead** regularly to encourage more blooms
- [x] **Remove** seed pods (unless saving seeds)
- [x] **Fertilize** after blooming with balanced fertilizer (10-10-10)
- [x] **Water** deeply during dry spells
- [x] **Divide** late-blooming varieties after they finish flowering

**What to Expect**:
- Late varieties continue blooming
- Foliage reaches full height
- Seed pods develop on un-deadheaded flowers
```

### ☀️ **Summer** (June - August)

#### Early Summer (June - Early July)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Water** deeply 1-2 times per week (1-2 inches total)
- [x] **Weed** regularly to reduce competition
- [x] **Monitor** for pests (iris borer, Japanese beetles)
- [x] **Remove** yellow or brown leaves
- [x] **Divide** reblooming irises after first bloom cycle

**What to Expect**:
- **Reblooming varieties** may produce second flush of flowers
- Foliage continues to grow and photosynthesize
- Rhizomes store energy for next year's blooms
```

#### Mid Summer (July)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Water** during drought (irises are drought-tolerant but bloom better with consistent moisture)
- [x] **Reduce watering** for bearded irises (they prefer drier conditions)
- [x] **Increase watering** for Siberian and Japanese irises (they prefer moist conditions)
- [x] **Fertilize** reblooming varieties lightly
- [x] **Pest control**: Watch for spider mites in hot, dry weather

**What to Expect**:
- Most irises enter **semi-dormancy** in hot weather
- Growth slows down
- Some varieties may go completely dormant
```

#### Late Summer (August)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Prepare for fall planting**: Order new rhizomes/bulbs
- [x] **Divide** clumps that need it (4-6 weeks after blooming)
- [x] **Reduce watering** to prepare plants for dormancy
- [x] **Clean up**: Remove any diseased or dead foliage
- [x] **Plan** for fall planting and bed renovation

**What to Expect**:
- **New rhizomes** begin to form
- Foliage may start to yellow and die back
- Plants begin **storing energy** for next year
```

### 🍂 **Fall** (September - November)

#### Early Fall (September)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Plant** new irises (6 weeks before first frost)
- [x] **Divide** existing clumps
- [x] **Fertilize**: Apply low-nitrogen fertilizer (5-10-10)
- [x] **Water** newly planted irises regularly
- [x] **Weed**: Remove weeds before they go to seed
- [x] **Pest control**: Apply nematodes for iris borer prevention

**What to Expect**:
- **Root growth** is most active in fall
- New plantings establish roots
- Existing plants **store energy** for spring blooming
```

#### Mid Fall (October)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Continue planting** (until 4-6 weeks before frost)
- [x] **Mulch**: Apply 2-3 inches of mulch after first frost
- [x] **Cut back** foliage to 6 inches (optional, helps prevent wind damage)
- [x] **Clean up**: Remove all dead foliage and debris
- [x] **Protect**: Cover tender varieties with extra mulch in cold climates

**What to Expect**:
- **Foliage** begins to die back
- Plants enter **dormancy**
- Root growth continues until soil freezes
```

#### Late Fall (November)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Final mulch application** (after several hard frosts)
- [x] **Protect** from freezing/thawing cycles
- [x] **Check** for heaving (rhizomes exposed by freeze-thaw)
- [x] **Plan** for next year's garden

**What to Expect**:
- Plants are **fully dormant**
- No visible growth above soil
- Rhizomes/bulbs **resting** underground
```

### ❄️ **Winter** (December - February)

#### Early Winter (December - January)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Monitor** mulch depth (maintain 2-3 inches)
- [x] **Check** for rodent damage (mice, voles)
- [x] **Remove** heavy snow from evergreen mulches
- [x] **Avoid** walking on frozen iris beds

**What to Expect**:
- Plants are **dormant**
- Rhizomes/bulbs **surviving** underground
- Minimal to no above-ground activity
```

#### Late Winter (February - Early March)

```md
**Tasks**:
- [x] **Check** for winter damage (heaving, rot)
- [x] **Remove** excess mulch gradually as temperatures rise
- [x] **Plan** for spring division and planting
- [x] **Order** new varieties for spring planting

**What to Expect**:
- **Early signs** of new growth in warm climates
- **Root activity** begins as soil warms
- **Preparation** for spring bloom season
```

## Care by Iris Type

### Bearded Irises

| **Season** | **Water** | **Fertilizer** | **Special Care** |
|------------|-----------|----------------|------------------|
| **Spring** | Moderate | Low-nitrogen (6-10-10) | Deadhead, stake tall varieties |
| **Summer** | Low to moderate | None | Reduce water in late summer |
| **Fall** | Moderate | Low-nitrogen (5-10-10) | Plant, divide, mulch after frost |
| **Winter** | None | None | Protect from heaving |

### Siberian Irises

| **Season** | **Water** | **Fertilizer** | **Special Care** |
|------------|-----------|----------------|------------------|
| **Spring** | High | Low-nitrogen (6-10-10) | Deadhead, divide if needed |
| **Summer** | High | None | Keep soil consistently moist |
| **Fall** | Moderate | Low-nitrogen (5-10-10) | Plant, divide, cut back foliage |
| **Winter** | None | None | Mulch heavily in cold climates |

### Japanese Irises

| **Season** | **Water** | **Fertilizer** | **Special Care** |
|------------|-----------|----------------|------------------|
| **Spring** | High | Low-nitrogen (6-10-10) | Deadhead, keep soil wet |

| **Fall** | Moderate | Low-nitrogen (5-10-10) | Plant, divide, cut back |
| **Winter** | None | None | Mulch, protect from freezing |

| **Season** | **Water** | **Fertilizer** | **Special Care** |

| **Winter** | None | None | Mulch to insulate |

## Watering Guide

### How Much to Water

| **Iris Type** | **Spring** | **Summer** | **Fall** | **Winter** |
|---------------|------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Bearded** | 1" per week | 1" every 2 weeks | 1" per week | None |
| **Siberian** | 1-2" per week | 1-2" per week | 1" per week | None |
| **Japanese** | 1-2" per week | 1-2" per week | 1" per week | None |
| **Louisiana** | 1-2" per week | 1-2" per week | 1" per week | None |
| **Bulbous** | 1" per week | None | 1" per week | None |

### Watering Tips

```md
**Best Watering Practices**:

✅ **DO**:
- Water **deeply and infrequently** (encourages deep root growth)
- Water **in the morning** (allows foliage to dry, prevents disease)
- Use **drip irrigation** or soaker hoses (keeps foliage dry)
- Water **at the base** of plants (not overhead)

❌ **DON'T**:
- **Overhead water** (can cause leaf spot and rot)
- Water **in the evening** (promotes disease)
- Use **sprinklers** that wet foliage
- **Overwater** (irises prefer slightly dry conditions)
```

### Signs of Water Stress

| **Problem** | **Symptoms** | **Solution** |
|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Underwatering** | Wilting, dry leaf tips, poor blooms | Water deeply, add mulch |
| **Overwatering** | Yellow leaves, soft rot, foul odor | Improve drainage, reduce watering |
| **Inconsistent Watering** | Uneven growth, bud blast | Water on regular schedule |

## Fertilizing Guide

### Fertilizer Types

| **Type** | **NPK Ratio** | **Best For** | **When to Use** | **Notes** |
|----------|---------------|--------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Low-Nitrogen** | 6-10-10 | Bearded irises | Early spring, fall | Promotes blooms, not foliage |
| **Balanced** | 10-10-10 | All irises | After blooming | General purpose |
| **High-Phosphorus** | 5-10-5 | Poor bloomers | Early spring | Encourages flowering |
| **Organic** | Varies | All irises | Any time | Slow release, improves soil |
| **Bone Meal** | 3-15-0 | Bulbous irises | Fall planting | Promotes root growth |

### Fertilizing Schedule

```
📅 Fertilizing Calendar:

**Early Spring** (March-April):
- Apply **low-nitrogen fertilizer** (6-10-10)
- Rate: **1/2 cup per plant** or **1 lb per 100 sq ft**
- Timing: As new growth appears

**After Blooming** (May-June):
- Apply **balanced fertilizer** (10-10-10)
- Rate: **1/4 cup per plant**
- Timing: After deadheading

**Fall** (September-October):
- Apply **low-nitrogen fertilizer** (5-10-10)
- Rate: **1/4 cup per plant**
- Timing: 4-6 weeks before first frost

**Never Fertilize**:
- Late summer (promotes soft growth)
- During dormancy
- Newly planted irises (wait 4-6 weeks)
```

### Organic Fertilizer Options

| **Fertilizer** | **NPK** | **Application Rate** | **Frequency** | **Notes** |
|---------------|---------|---------------------|---------------|-----------|
| **Compost** | Varies | 1-2 inches as mulch | Annually | Improves soil structure |
| **Well-rotted Manure** | 1-1-1 | 1 inch mixed in | Annually | Don't use fresh manure |
| **Worm Castings** | 1-0-0 | 1/4 cup per plant | Monthly | Gentle, won't burn |
| **Fish Emulsion** | 5-1-1 | 1 tbsp per gallon | Monthly | Fast acting, smelly |
| **Seaweed/Kelp** | 1-0-2 | 1 tbsp per gallon | Monthly | Adds micronutrients |
| **Bone Meal** | 3-15-0 | 1/4 cup per plant | Fall | For bulbous irises |

## Pest & Disease Management

### Common Pests

#### 🐛 **Iris Borer** (*Macronoctua onusta*)

**Symptoms:**
- Ragged, chewed leaves
- Tunnels in rhizomes
- Soft, mushy rhizomes
- Wilting, yellowing foliage

**Prevention:**
- [x] Remove and destroy **old foliage in fall**
- [x] **Divide clumps** regularly (every 3-4 years)
- [x] **Clean up** garden debris
- [x] Apply **beneficial nematodes** in fall and spring
- [x] **Solarize** soil in infested areas

**Treatment:**
- Dig up and **destroy** infested rhizomes
- Apply **Bacillus thuringiensis** (Bt) to foliage
- Use **insecticidal soap** for young larvae

#### 🦟 **Aphids**

**Symptoms:**
- Curled, distorted leaves
- Sticky residue (honeydew)
- Sooty mold on leaves

**Prevention:**
- [x] **Encourage** beneficial insects (ladybugs, lacewings)
- [x] **Plant** companion plants (marigolds, nasturtiums)
- [x] **Avoid** over-fertilizing with nitrogen

**Treatment:**
- Spray with **strong water stream**
- Apply **insecticidal soap** or **neem oil**
- Introduce **ladybugs**

#### 🐞 **Thrips**

**Symptoms:**
- Silvery streaks on leaves
- Distorted growth
- Reduced flowering

**Prevention:**
- [x] **Remove** weeds and debris
- [x] **Avoid** overwatering
- [x] **Use** reflective mulch

**Treatment:**
- Apply **neem oil** or **insecticidal soap**
- Use **sticky traps**
- Introduce **predatory mites**

#### 🦌 **Deer & Rabbits**

**Symptoms:**
- Eaten foliage and flowers
- Trampled plants

**Prevention:**
- [x] **Fence** garden (8 ft tall for deer)
- [x] **Plant** deer-resistant varieties
- [x] Use **repellents** (commercial or homemade)
- [x] **Interplant** with deer-resistant plants

**Treatment:**
- Apply **commercial repellents**
- Use **motion-activated sprinklers**
- **Hand-pick** pests (for small gardens)

### Common Diseases

#### 🍄 **Bacterial Soft Rot** (*Erwinia carotovora*)

**Symptoms:**
- Soft, mushy rhizomes
- Foul odor
- Yellow, wilting foliage

**Prevention:**
- [x] **Improve** drainage
- [x] **Avoid** overhead watering
- [x] **Sterilize** tools between cuts
- [x] **Remove** and destroy infected plants

**Treatment:**
- **No effective treatment** - remove and destroy infected plants
- **Solarize** soil in infested areas

#### 🍄 **Leaf Spot** (*Didymellina macrospora*)

**Symptoms:**
- Small, brown spots on leaves
- Spots may have **yellow halos**
- Severe cases: **defoliation**

**Prevention:**
- [x] **Remove** and destroy infected leaves
- [x] **Avoid** overhead watering
- [x] **Improve** air circulation
- [x] **Clean up** debris in fall

**Treatment:**
- Apply **copper fungicide**
- Remove **infected leaves**
- **Improve** growing conditions

#### 🍄 **Root Rot** (*Phytophthora, Pythium*)

**Symptoms:**
- Yellowing, wilting foliage
- Stunted growth
- Soft, brown roots

**Prevention:**
- [x] **Improve** drainage
- [x] **Avoid** overwatering
- [x] **Plant** in well-draining soil
- [x] **Use** raised beds in wet areas

**Treatment:**
- **No effective treatment** - remove and destroy infected plants
- **Solarize** or **fumigate** soil

## Winter Protection

### By Climate Zone

| **Zone** | **Winter Low** | **Protection Needed** | **Recommended Methods** |
|----------|----------------|-----------------------|------------------------|
| **3-4** | -40°F to -30°F | High | 4-6" mulch + evergreen boughs |
| **5-6** | -20°F to -10°F | Moderate | 3-4" mulch |
| **7-8** | 0°F to 10°F | Low | 2-3" mulch |
| **9-11** | 20°F to 40°F | None | None needed |

### Winter Protection Methods

```md
**Mulching**:
- **Materials**: Straw, pine needles, shredded leaves
- **Depth**: 2-4 inches (6 inches in very cold climates)
- **Timing**: After **several hard frosts** (not too early)
- **Technique**: Keep mulch **loose** (not packed down)

**Evergreen Boughs**:
- **Materials**: Pine, spruce, or fir boughs
- **Method**: Lay boughs **over plants** after mulching
- **Benefit**: Traps snow for insulation

**Row Covers**:
- **Materials**: Burlap, frost cloth, old blankets
- **Method**: Drape over plants, secure with stakes
- **Timing**: During **extreme cold snaps**

**Cloche**:
- **Materials**: Glass jars, plastic containers
- **Method**: Place over individual plants
- **Use**: For **small plantings** or special varieties
```

### Protecting from Freeze-Thaw Cycles

```md
**Problem**: Alternating freezing and thawing can **heave** rhizomes out of the soil.

**Solutions**:
- Apply **thick mulch** (4-6 inches) after first frost
- Use **evergreen boughs** to trap snow
- **Press down** any exposed rhizomes
- **Avoid** walking on beds when soil is frozen
```

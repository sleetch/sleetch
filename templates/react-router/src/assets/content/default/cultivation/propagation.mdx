# Propagation Techniques

> **"One iris becomes ten, ten become a hundred - the gardener's miracle of multiplication."**

Propagation is the **key to expanding your iris collection** and sharing these beautiful plants with others. Irises can be propagated through **several methods**, each with its own advantages and best practices.

## Why Propagate Irises?

### Benefits of Propagation

✅ **Expand your collection** without buying new plants
✅ **Rejuvenate old clumps** that have stopped blooming well
✅ **Share with friends** and fellow gardeners
✅ **Preserve heirloom varieties** that may be hard to find
✅ **Create new hybrids** through cross-pollination
✅ **Save money** on new plants
✅ **Gift unique plants** to others

### When to Propagate

| **Method** | **Best Time** | **Alternative Times** | **Avoid** |
|------------|---------------|----------------------|-----------|
| **Division** | 4-6 weeks after blooming | Early spring | During bloom, late fall |
| **Seeds** | Fall (after collection) | Early spring (indoors) | Winter, summer heat |
| **Tissue Culture** | Any time (sterile conditions) | - | Contaminated conditions |

## Method 1: Division (Most Common)

Division is the **easiest and most reliable** method for propagating irises. It involves **separating the rhizomes** of an established clump into individual or smaller groupings.

### When to Divide

#### Best Times by Region

| **Region** | **Primary Time** | **Secondary Time** | **Notes** |
|------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Cold Climates** (Zones 3-5) | Late summer (Aug-Sept) | Early spring | Allow 6 weeks before frost |
| **Moderate Climates** (Zones 6-8) | Early fall (Sept-Oct) | Early spring | Avoid summer heat |
| **Warm Climates** (Zones 9-11) | Fall (Oct-Nov) | Late winter | Avoid summer |

#### Signs It's Time to Divide

- [x] **Reduced blooming** (fewer flowers than usual)
- [x] **Smaller blooms** (flowers are not as large)
- [x] **Center die-out** (dead area in middle of clump)
- [x] **Overcrowding** (rhizomes growing on top of each other)
- [x] **Every 3-4 years** (preventative maintenance)

### Step-by-Step Division Guide

#### Tools Needed

```md
**Essential Tools**:
- Sharp knife or garden spade
- Garden fork
- Pruning shears
- Trowel
- Bucket of water (optional)
- Root stimulator (optional)
- Labels and marker

**Optional Tools**:
- Garden hose (for cleaning rhizomes)
- Disinfectant (bleach or rubbing alcohol)
- Scale (for measuring divisions)
- Plastic bags (for storage)
```

#### Division Process

```md
**Step 1: Prepare the Clump**
1. Water the clump **1-2 days before dividing** (makes digging easier)
2. Cut back foliage to **6-8 inches** (reduces stress on plant)
3. Gather all necessary tools
4. Prepare new planting holes (if replanting immediately)

**Step 2: Lift the Clump**
1. Insert garden fork **12 inches away** from clump
2. Gently **pry up** the entire clump
3. Lift **carefully** to avoid breaking rhizomes
4. Shake off **excess soil** to expose rhizomes
5. Rinse with water if needed to **see roots clearly**

**Step 3: Separate the Rhizomes**
1. Identify **natural divisions** (where rhizomes naturally separate)
2. Use **hands** to gently pull apart (for loose clumps)
3. Use **knife** to cut between rhizomes (for tight clumps)
4. **Disinfect knife** between cuts (prevents disease spread)
5. Ensure each division has:
   - At least **1 healthy fan** of leaves
   - A **portion of rhizome**
   - **Healthy roots**

**Step 4: Trim and Prepare Divisions**
1. Trim **leaves** to 6 inches (reduces water loss)
2. Remove **dead or damaged roots**
3. Check for **pests or disease** (discard infected divisions)
4. Soak in **water for 1-2 hours** (optional, helps rehydrate)
5. Dip in **root stimulator** (optional, encourages root growth)

**Step 5: Replant Divisions**
1. Plant at **same depth** as original (top of rhizome visible)
2. Space **12-24 inches apart** (closer for dwarf varieties)
3. Water **thoroughly** after planting
4. Keep soil **moist for first 2 weeks**
5. **Do NOT fertilize** for 4-6 weeks
```

### Division Patterns

#### For Bearded Irises

```
Clump Structure:
├── Mother Rhizome (oldest, may be spent)
├── Daughter Rhizomes (1-2 years old, best for division)
└── Granddaughter Rhizomes (newest, may be too small)

**Best Divisions**:
- Use **daughter rhizomes** (1-2 years old)
- Each should have **1-2 fans** of leaves
- Discard **mother rhizome** if it's old and woody
```

#### For Siberian and Japanese Irises

```
Clump Structure:
├── Older divisions (center, may be less vigorous)
└── Younger divisions (outer edges, most vigorous)

**Best Divisions**:
- Use **outer divisions** (most vigorous)
- Each should have **2-3 fans** of leaves
- Can divide **more frequently** (every 2-3 years)
```

### Division Size Guide

| **Division Size** | **Fans per Division** | **Rhizome Age** | **Bloom Time** | **Best For** |
|-------------------|-----------------------|----------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Large** | 3-5 fans | 2-3 years | Next year | Immediate impact |
| **Medium** | 2-3 fans | 1-2 years | 1-2 years | Most common |
| **Small** | 1-2 fans | 1 year | 2-3 years | Maximizing stock |
| **Single Fan** | 1 fan | New growth | 2-4 years | Breeding, rare varieties |

### Aftercare for Divisions

```md
**First 2 Weeks**:
- [x] Keep soil **consistently moist** (not soggy)
- [x] **Shade** from hot afternoon sun (if very hot)
- [x] **Monitor** for stress or wilting
- [ ] **Do NOT fertilize**

**First Month**:
- [x] Water **weekly** (1 inch per week)
- [x] **Remove** any dead or yellowing leaves
- [x] **Weed** around new plantings
- [ ] **Do NOT fertilize**

**First Year**:
- [x] Water **during dry spells**
- [x] **Mulch** lightly in winter
- [x] **Fertilize** lightly in spring
- [ ] **Do NOT divide** again
```

### Storing Divisions (If Not Planting Immediately)

```md
**Short-Term Storage** (1-2 weeks):
1. Keep rhizomes **slightly moist** (not wet)
2. Store in **shade** (not direct sun)
3. Maintain **cool temperatures** (50-60°F ideal)
4. Check **daily** for mold or rot

**Long-Term Storage** (up to 1 month):
1. **Do NOT store** if possible (best to plant immediately)
2. If necessary, **heel in** (temporarily plant in soil)
3. Keep in **moist sand or peat moss**
4. Store in **cool, dark place**
5. Check **weekly** for mold or drying out
```

## Method 2: Seed Propagation

Growing irises from seed is **rewarding but requires patience**. Seed-grown irises may take **2-5 years** to bloom and may not be **true to the parent plant** (unless it's a species, not a hybrid).

### Seed Collection

#### When to Collect Seeds

```md
**Seed Development Timeline**:
- **Pollination**: Spring (during bloom)
- **Seed Pod Formation**: 2-3 weeks after pollination
- **Seed Maturation**: 6-8 weeks after pollination
- **Harvest Time**: When pod turns **brown and begins to split**
```

#### How to Collect Seeds

```md
**Steps**:
1. **Identify mature pods**: Look for **brown, dry pods** that are starting to split
2. **Collect pods**: Cut or gently twist pods from plant
3. **Dry pods**: Place in **paper bag** in warm, dry location
4. **Extract seeds**: Once fully dry, pods will **split open**
5. **Clean seeds**: Remove any **pod debris**
6. **Store seeds**: Keep in **paper envelope** in cool, dry place

**Storage**:
- **Temperature**: 40-50°F (refrigerator works well)
- **Humidity**: Low (use silica gel packets if needed)
- **Duration**: Up to **2-3 years** (viability decreases over time)
```

### Seed Stratification

Many iris seeds require **cold stratification** (a period of cold, moist conditions) to germinate.

#### Stratification Methods

| **Method** | **Process** | **Duration** | **Best For** |
|------------|-------------|--------------|-------------|
| **Cold Moist** | Mix seeds with moist sand, refrigerate | 4-8 weeks | Most irises |
| **Winter Sowing** | Plant outdoors in fall, let nature stratify | Winter | Hardiest varieties |
| **Freezer** | Freeze seeds for 1-2 weeks, then refrigerate | 2-4 weeks | Some species |
| **Outdoor** | Plant in fall, let winter provide stratification | Winter | Cold climates |

#### Cold Moist Stratification (Most Reliable)

```md
**Steps**:
1. Mix seeds with **moist sand or peat moss** (not wet)
2. Place in **sealed plastic bag** or container
3. Label with **date and variety**
4. Refrigerate at **35-40°F**
5. Check **weekly** for mold or germination
6. **Duration**: 4-8 weeks (varies by species)

**After Stratification**:
- Seeds are ready to plant when they **begin to swell**
- Some may **start germinating** in the bag
- Plant **immediately** if germination begins
```

### Sowing Seeds

#### Indoor Sowing (Recommended for Most)

```md
**Materials Needed**:
- Seed trays or small pots
- Seed starting mix (sterile, well-draining)
- Plastic wrap or humidity dome
- Grow lights or sunny window
- Heat mat (optional)

**Steps**:
1. **Prepare containers**: Fill with **moist seed starting mix**
2. **Sow seeds**: Place seeds **1/4 inch deep**
3. **Cover lightly**: With **fine soil or vermiculite**
4. **Water gently**: Use **spray bottle** to avoid disturbing seeds
5. **Cover**: With **plastic wrap** to maintain humidity
6. **Place**: In **warm location** (60-70°F)
7. **Light**: Provide **bright, indirect light**

**Germination**:
- **Time**: 2-8 weeks (varies by species)
- **Temperature**: 60-70°F ideal
- **Light**: Bright but not direct sun
- **Moisture**: Keep **consistently moist** (not soggy)
```

#### Outdoor Sowing

```md
**When to Sow Outdoors**:
- **Fall**: Best for cold stratification (zones 3-7)
- **Early Spring**: After last frost (zones 8-11)

**Steps**:
1. **Prepare bed**: Loosen soil, remove weeds
2. **Sow seeds**: Plant **1/4 inch deep**, 2-3 inches apart
3. **Cover**: With **fine soil**
4. **Water**: Gently to settle soil
5. **Mark**: Location with labels (seeds are tiny!)
6. **Mulch**: Lightly with **straw or fine bark**

**Aftercare**:
- Keep soil **moist but not soggy**
- **Weed** carefully (seedlings are small)
- **Thin** seedlings to 6 inches apart when they have 2-3 leaves
```

### Seedling Care

```md
**First Year**:
- [x] Keep in **partial shade** (protect from hot sun)
- [x] Water **regularly** (keep soil moist)
- [x] **Fertilize lightly** with diluted fertilizer (1/4 strength)
- [x] **Transplant** to individual pots when they have 2-3 true leaves
- [ ] **Do NOT expect blooms** (focus on leaf growth)

**Second Year**:
- [x] **Harden off** seedlings before transplanting outdoors
- [x] Plant in **garden** in spring or fall
- [x] **Fertilize** with low-nitrogen fertilizer
- [x] **Water** regularly during first growing season
- [ ] **May see first blooms** (but don't be disappointed if not)

**Third Year+**:
- [x] **Treat as mature plants**
- [x] **Full blooms** should appear
- [x] **Divide** if clumps become overcrowded
```

### Seed Propagation by Species

| **Species** | **Stratification Needed** | **Germination Time** | **Bloom Time** | **Difficulty** |
|-------------|---------------------------|---------------------|---------------|----------------|
| *Iris germanica* | Yes (4-6 weeks) | 2-4 weeks | 3-5 years | Medium |
| *Iris versicolor* | Yes (6-8 weeks) | 3-6 weeks | 2-3 years | Easy |
| *Iris pseudacorus* | Yes (4-6 weeks) | 2-4 weeks | 2-3 years | Easy |
| *Iris cristata* | Yes (6-8 weeks) | 4-8 weeks | 2-3 years | Medium |
| *Iris reticulata* | Yes (8-12 weeks) | 4-8 weeks | 2-3 years | Medium |
| *Iris setosa* | No | 1-2 weeks | 2-3 years | Easy |
| *Iris ensata* | Yes (4-6 weeks) | 2-4 weeks | 2-3 years | Easy |

## Method 3: Tissue Culture (Advanced)

Tissue culture is a **laboratory technique** for propagating irises **aseptically** (without disease). It's used primarily by **commercial growers** and **breeders** to produce large numbers of identical plants.

### When to Use Tissue Culture

✅ **Mass propagation** of valuable cultivars
✅ **Disease elimination** from infected plants
✅ **Rapid multiplication** of new hybrids
✅ **Preservation** of rare or endangered species
✅ **Year-round propagation** (not season-dependent)

❌ **Not for beginners** (requires sterile conditions and equipment)
❌ **Expensive** (specialized equipment and supplies)
❌ **Time-consuming** (requires regular attention)

### Tissue Culture Steps

```md
**Step 1: Explant Selection**
- Choose **healthy, disease-free** plant material
- Use **young, actively growing** tissue
- **Rhizome sections**: 1-2 cm pieces with bud
- **Leaf bases**: 2-3 cm sections
- **Shoot tips**: 1-2 cm with growing point

**Step 2: Surface Sterilization**
1. Rinse explants in **running water** for 5 minutes
2. Dip in **70% ethanol** for 30 seconds
3. Transfer to **10% bleach + 1 drop Tween 20** for 10 minutes
4. Rinse **3 times** with sterile distilled water
5. **Optional**: Second sterilization with 5% bleach for 5 minutes

**Step 3: Inoculation**
1. Work in **laminar flow hood** or sterile environment
2. Use **sterile tools** (flamed between uses)
3. Place explants on **initiation medium**
4. Seal containers with **Parafilm or plastic wrap**
5. Label with **date, variety, and stage**

**Step 4: Incubation**
- Place in **growth chamber** or under grow lights
- Maintain **22-25°C**
- Provide **16h photoperiod**
- Keep **high humidity** (80-90%)

**Step 5: Multiplication**
- After **4-6 weeks**, transfer to **multiplication medium**
- Subculture every **4-6 weeks**
- Separate **clusters** into individual plantlets

**Step 6: Rooting**
- Transfer to **rooting medium**
- Reduce **cytokinin** concentration
- Increase **auxin** concentration
- Allow **4-6 weeks** for root development

**Step 7: Hardening**
- Gradually **reduce humidity**
- Increase **light intensity**
- Open containers **slightly** to acclimate
- Prepare for **transplanting**

**Step 8: Transplanting**
- Transfer to **soil mix** (sterile potting mix)
- Keep in **high humidity** for 1-2 weeks
- Gradually **acclimate** to normal conditions
- Plant in **garden** after 4-6 weeks
```

### Tissue Culture Media Recipes

```
**MS Medium (Murashige and Skoog) Base**:
├── Macroelements

├── Microelements
│   ├── FeSO4·7H2O: 27.8 mg/L
│   ├── MnSO4·H2O: 22.3 mg/L
│   ├── ZnSO4·7H2O: 8.6 mg/L
│   ├── H3BO3: 6.2 mg/L
│   ├── KI: 0.83 mg/L
│   ├── Na2MoO4·2H2O: 0.25 mg/L
│   ├── CuSO4·5H2O: 0.025 mg/L

├── Vitamins

**Initiation Medium**:
- MS medium + 2,4-D (1 mg/L) + kinetin (0.5 mg/L)
- Solidified with agar (7 g/L)

**Multiplication Medium**:
- MS medium + BAP (1 mg/L) + NAA (0.1 mg/L)
- Solidified with agar (7 g/L)

**Rooting Medium**:
- 1/2 MS medium + IBA (0.5 mg/L)
- Solidified with agar (7 g/L)
```

## Method 4: Micropropagation (For Bulbous Irises)

Micropropagation is similar to tissue culture but **specifically adapted for bulbous irises** like reticulata and Dutch irises.

### Bulb Scale Propagation

```md
**Steps**:
1. **Select bulbs**: Choose **large, healthy bulbs**
2. **Remove scales**: Peel off **outer scales** (keep inner scales intact)
3. **Sterilize**: Dip in **10% bleach** for 5 minutes, rinse
4. **Culture**: Place scales on **bulb medium** (MS + BA + NAA)
5. **Incubate**: 20-25°C, dark for 4 weeks, then light
6. **Develop**: Small **bulblets** form at base of scales
7. **Transplant**: After 8-12 weeks, plant bulblets in soil
```

## Propagation Troubleshooting

### Common Problems and Solutions

| **Problem** | **Cause** | **Solution** |
|-------------|-----------|--------------|
| **No growth after division** | Shock, poor roots | Keep moist, provide shade, be patient |
| **Rotting divisions** | Too wet, poor drainage | Improve drainage, reduce watering |
| **Seeds not germinating** | Old seeds, no stratification | Use fresh seeds, stratify properly |
| **Seedlings damping off** | Overwatering, fungus | Use sterile mix, water carefully |
| **Tissue culture contamination** | Poor sterilization | Improve sterile technique, use antibiotics |
| **Slow growth in divisions** | Poor soil, lack of nutrients | Amend soil, fertilize lightly |
| **No blooms in seed-grown plants** | Immature, poor conditions | Be patient, improve growing conditions |

### Propagation Success Rates

| **Method** | **Success Rate** | **Time to Bloom** | **Difficulty** | **Cost** |
|------------|------------------|-------------------|---------------|---------|
| **Division** | 90-95% | 1-2 years | Easy | Low |
| **Seeds** | 60-80% | 2-5 years | Medium | Low |
| **Tissue Culture** | 80-90% | 1-2 years | Hard | High |
| **Micropropagation** | 70-85% | 1-2 years | Hard | High |

## Tips for Propagation Success

### General Tips

```md
✅ **DO**:
- Use **sharp, clean tools** for division
- **Label** all divisions and seedlings
- **Keep records** of parentage and dates
- **Be patient** - some methods take years
- **Start small** - don't try to propagate everything at once

❌ **DON'T**:
- **Rush** the process (take your time)
- **Overwater** new divisions or seedlings
- **Fertilize** immediately after division
- **Divide** during bloom or extreme weather
- **Give up** if first attempts fail
```

### Propagation Calendar

```
📅 Monthly Propagation Tasks:

**January-February**:
- Start seeds indoors (with stratification)
- Tissue culture work (indoors)
- Plan division schedule

**March-April**:
- Direct sow seeds outdoors (cold climates)
- Divide early-blooming varieties (after blooming)
- Harden off indoor seedlings

**May-June**:
- Divide most varieties (4-6 weeks after blooming)
- Collect seeds from early bloomers
- Transplant seedlings

**July-August**:
- Divide late-blooming varieties
- Collect and stratify seeds
- Prepare for fall planting

**September-October**:
- Divide remaining varieties
- Plant stratified seeds
- Take cuttings (some species)

**November-December**:
- Sow seeds outdoors for natural stratification
- Tissue culture work (indoors)
- Review and plan for next year
```

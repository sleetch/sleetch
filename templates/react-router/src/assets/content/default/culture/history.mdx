# Historical Uses & Timeline

> **"The history of the iris is the history of civilization itself - from ancient medicine to modern gardens, this flower has walked beside humanity for millennia."**

The iris has played a **remarkable role in human history**, serving as **medicine, food, dye, perfume, and symbol** across cultures and centuries. This timeline explores the **fascinating journey** of the iris through human civilization.

## Ancient History (Pre-500 CE)

### Prehistoric Uses

**Archaeological Evidence:**
- **30,000 BCE**: Iris pollen found in **Neanderthal burial sites** (Shanidar Cave, Iraq)
- **10,000 BCE**: Iris seeds discovered in **Mesolithic settlements** (Europe)
- **5,000 BCE**: Iris images appear in **Neolithic cave paintings** (France, Spain)

**Early Uses:**
- **Food**: Iris roots were **roasted and eaten**
- **Medicine**: Iris was used in **prehistoric healing rituals**
- **Fiber**: Iris leaves were used for **cordage and weaving**
- **Dye**: Iris petals provided **natural pigments**

### Ancient Egypt (3100 BCE - 332 BCE)

#### Medicinal Uses

The **Ebers Papyrus** (1550 BCE), one of the oldest medical texts, mentions iris:

```md
**Ebers Papyrus Remedies with Iris**:

1. **Digestive Aid**:
   - "Iris root, crushed and mixed with honey, for stomach pains"
   - Used to treat **indigestion and bloating**

2. **Skin Treatment**:
   - "Iris root paste for wounds and skin irritations"
   - Used as an **anti-inflammatory**

3. **Respiratory Relief**:
   - "Iris root tea for coughs and breathing difficulties"
   - Used as an **expectorant**

4. **Women's Health**:
   - "Iris preparations for menstrual disorders"
   - Used to **regulate cycles**
```

#### Cosmetic Uses

> **"The eyes of Nefertiti were lined with the darkest kohl, made from the finest iris petals ground with galena."**

**Egyptian Cosmetics:**
- **Kohl**: Ground iris petals mixed with **galena (lead sulfide)** for eye makeup
- **Perfume**: Iris oil was used in **kyphi** (sacred incense)
- **Hair Dye**: Iris extracts were used to **color hair**
- **Skin Care**: Iris paste was used as a **facial treatment**

#### Religious and Funerary Uses

```
Iris in Egyptian Religion:
├── **Temple Offerings**: Iris flowers were placed on **altars to the gods**
├── **Burial Goods**: Iris was included in **tomb offerings**
├── **Amulets**: Iris-shaped amulets **protected the deceased**
├── **Mummification**: Iris oil was used in **embalming processes**
└── **Divination**: Iris was used in **oracle rituals**
```

**Famous Egyptian Iris References:**
- **Tutankhamun's Tomb**: Iris **pollen found in burial chamber**
- **Temple of Isis**: Iris **carvings and paintings**
- **Book of the Dead**: Iris **mentioned in spells**

### Ancient Mesopotamia (3500 BCE - 539 BCE)

#### Sumerian and Babylonian Uses

**Cuneiform Tablets** mention iris in various contexts:

```md
**Mesopotamian Uses of Iris**:

1. **Medicine**:
   - "Iris root for digestive ailments" (Nippur medical tablet, 2100 BCE)
   - "Iris paste for skin diseases" (Babylonian medical text)

2. **Agriculture**:
   - Iris was **cultivated in temple gardens**
   - Used in **irrigation rituals**

3. **Magic and Divination**:
   - Iris was used in **divination rituals**
   - Iris petals were **scattered for omens**

4. **Perfumery**:
   - Iris oil was a **valuable trade commodity**
   - Used in **temple incense**
```

#### The Hanging Gardens of Babylon

> **"Among the exotic plants of the Hanging Gardens, the iris stood tall, its sword-like leaves catching the sunlight like the blades of the king's guard."**

- Iris was likely **one of the plants** in the legendary gardens
- Its **drought tolerance** made it suitable for the **arid climate**
- Its **beauty** made it a favorite of **King Nebuchadnezzar**

### Ancient Greece (800 BCE - 146 BCE)

#### Hippocratic Medicine

The **Hippocratic Corpus** (4th century BCE) includes several references to iris:

```md
**Hippocratic Remedies with Iris**:

1. **For Wounds**:
   - "Iris root, crushed and applied as a poultice, stops bleeding"

2. **For Digestive Issues**:
   - "Iris root tea for stomach pains and diarrhea"

3. **For Women's Health**:
   - "Iris preparations to induce menstruation"

4. **For Skin Conditions**:
   - "Iris paste for eczema and rashes"
```

#### Theophrastus: The Father of Botany

**Theophrastus** (371-287 BCE), a student of Aristotle, wrote about iris in his botanical works:

```md
**Theophrastus on Iris** (from *Enquiry into Plants*):

- **Classification**: Described iris as a **"plant with sword-like leaves"**
- **Habitat**: Noted it grows in **"dry, sunny places"**
- **Medicinal Uses**: Mentioned its use for **"wounds and digestive ailments"**
- **Cultivation**: Described **propagation methods**
- **Varieties**: Noted **different colors and forms**
```

#### Dioscorides: De Materia Medica

**Pedanius Dioscorides** (40-90 CE) included iris in his **pharmacopeia**:

```md
**Dioscorides on Iris** (from *De Materia Medica*):

- **Iris germanica**: "The root is good for wounds and digestive issues"
- **Iris pseudacorus**: "The yellow iris is used for skin conditions"
- **Preparation**: "The root should be dried and powdered"
- **Dosage**: "A drachm (3.5g) of the powder in wine"
```

### Ancient Rome (753 BCE - 476 CE)

#### Pliny the Elder: Natural History

**Pliny the Elder** (23-79 CE) wrote extensively about iris in his *Natural History*:

```md
**Pliny on Iris** (from *Natural History*, Book 21):

- **Name**: "The Greeks call it iris, from the rainbow"
- **Medicinal Uses**: "The root is astringent and good for wounds"
- **Cosmetic Uses**: "The juice is used by women for freckles"
- **Magical Uses**: "The root is used in love potions"
- **Cultivation**: "It grows best in dry, sunny places"
```

#### Roman Agricultural Writers

**Columella** (4-70 CE) and **Varro** (116-27 BCE) wrote about iris cultivation:

```md
**Roman Iris Cultivation**:

- **Soil**: "Iris prefers **light, well-drained soil**"
- **Water**: "It requires **little water** once established"
- **Propagation**: "Divide the roots in **autumn or early spring**"
- **Uses**: "The flowers are used for **garlands and decorations**"
```

#### Roman Medicine

**Galen** (129-216 CE), the famous Roman physician, used iris in his remedies:

```md
**Galen's Iris Remedies**:

1. **For Digestive Issues**:
   - "Iris root powder in honey for stomach pains"

2. **For Wounds**:
   - "Iris root poultice to stop bleeding"

3. **For Skin Conditions**:
   - "Iris paste for eczema and rashes"

4. **For Respiratory Issues**:
   - "Iris root tea for coughs"
```

**Charlemagne's** (742-814 CE) *Capitulare de Villis* (estate regulations) mentions iris:

```
**New Iris Discoveries**:

- **1500s**: Spanish explorers bring **New World irises** to Europe
```

## Early Modern Period (1700-1900 CE)

### The Age of Enlightenment

#### Carl Linnaeus

**Carl Linnaeus** (1707-1778 CE) established the **modern classification** of iris:

```md
**Linnaeus and Iris**:

- **Binomial Nomenclature**: Established the name **Iris** for the genus
- **Classification**: Placed iris in the **family Iridaceae**
- **Species**: Described **many iris species** in *Species Plantarum* (1753)
- **Type Species**: Designated **Iris germanica** as the type species
```

#### The Birth of Modern Botany

**Botanical expeditions** in the 18th and 19th centuries discovered **new iris species**:

```
**Botanical Expeditions**:

- **1700s**: **Carl Peter Thunberg** discovers iris species in **South Africa**
- **1800s**: **Alexander von Humboldt** collects iris species in **South America**
- **1800s**: **Joseph Banks** brings iris species from **Australia**
- **1800s**: **Asa Gray** describes **North American iris species**
```

## Modern Era (1900-Present)

### The 20th Century: The Age of Hybridization

#### The Birth of Modern Iris Breeding

The **early 20th century** saw the **birth of modern iris breeding**:

```
**Pioneers of Iris Breeding**:

- **William R. Dykes** (1877-1925): Wrote *The Genus Iris*, established modern classification
- **Arthur J. Bliss** (1860-1941): Developed many **bearded iris cultivars**
- **Sidney B. Mitchell** (1878-1947): Created **award-winning iris hybrids**
- **Grace Sturtevant** (1865-1947): First woman to **register iris hybrids**
```

#### The American Iris Society

The **American Iris Society (AIS)** was founded in **1920**:

```md
**AIS Milestones**:

- **1920**: Founded in **Philadelphia, Pennsylvania**
- **1921**: First **iris show** held
- **1922**: First **iris registry** established
- **1925**: First **iris journal** (*Bulletin of the American Iris Society*) published
- **1930s**: **Hybrid registration** system established
- **1950s**: **Median iris** classification developed
- **1970s**: **Reblooming iris** breeding program initiated
```

#### The Iris in the 21st Century

**Modern developments in iris cultivation and research:**

```
**21st Century Iris Developments**:

- **Genetic Research**: Iris **genome sequencing** projects
- **Tissue Culture**: **Mass propagation** of iris cultivars
- **Disease Resistance**: Breeding for **resistance to iris borer and other pests**
- **New Hybrids**: Development of **new colors, patterns, and forms**
- **Conservation**: Efforts to **preserve wild iris species**
- **Global Trade**: Iris **rhizomes and bulbs** traded internationally
```

## Economic History

### The Iris Trade

The **commercial iris trade** has a long history:

```
**Iris Trade Timeline**:

- **1500s**: Iris **rhizomes traded** in European markets
- **1600s**: **Dutch iris bulbs** become a trade commodity
- **1800s**: **American iris nurseries** established
- **1900s**: **International iris trade** develops
- **2000s**: **Online iris sales** become popular
```

### The Perfume Industry

Iris has been **valuable in the perfume industry** for centuries:

```md
**Iris in Perfumery**:

- **Orris Root**: The **dried rhizome** of iris (primarily *Iris germanica* and *Iris pallida*)
- **Extraction**: Orris root is **aged for 3-5 years** to develop its fragrance
- **Scent**: Orris has a **violet-like, powdery, woody** scent
- **Use**: Used as a **fixative** in perfumes (helps other scents last longer)
- **Value**: Orris root is **one of the most expensive** natural perfume ingredients

**Famous Perfumes with Iris/Orris**:
- **Chanel No. 5**: Contains orris root
- **Guerlain Shalimar**: Features iris notes
- **Diorissimo**: Iris is a key ingredient
- **Hermès Hiris**: Named after the iris
```

### The Dye Industry

Iris has been used as a **source of natural dyes**:

```md
**Iris Dyes**:

- **Purple/Blue**: From **iris petals** (anthocyanins)
- **Yellow**: From **iris roots** (flavonoids)
- **Green**: From **iris leaves** (chlorophyll)
- **Use**: Used to dye **fabrics, yarns, and other materials**

**Historical Dye Uses**:
- **Ancient Egypt**: Used for **cosmetics and fabrics**
- **Medieval Europe**: Used in **manuscript illumination**
- **Renaissance**: Used for **textile dyeing**
- **Victorian Era**: Used in **crafts and decorations**
```

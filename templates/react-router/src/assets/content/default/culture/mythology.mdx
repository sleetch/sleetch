# Mythology & Symbolism

> **"The iris is the oracle of the gods, carrying messages between heaven and earth on the wings of its petals."**
> — Ancient Interpretation

The iris has been **revered, mythologized, and symbolized** across cultures and millennia. Its name itself comes from the Greek word for **rainbow**, connecting it to the divine messenger of the gods.

## Greek Mythology: The Rainbow Goddess

### Iris: The Divine Messenger

In **Greek mythology**, Iris (Ἶρις) was the **goddess of the rainbow** and the **personification of the rainbow itself**. She served as a **messenger between the gods and humanity**, traveling with the speed of the wind from one end of the earth to the other.

```
Iris in Greek Mythology:
├── **Parentage**: Daughter of Thaumas (marvel) and Electra (amber)
├── **Siblings**: The Harpies (storm spirits)
├── **Role**: Divine messenger, goddess of the rainbow
├── **Attributes**: Wings, caduceus (herald's staff), golden sandals
├── **Symbol**: The rainbow (her namesake)
└── **Worship**: Revered as a bridge between heaven and earth
```

### The Myth of Iris

According to **Homer's Iliad**, Iris was one of the **Olympian goddesses** who could travel freely between the worlds of gods and mortals:

> **"Then Iris, fleet as the wind, descended from high Olympus to bring the message to Priam in sacred Ilios."**
> — Homer, *Iliad* Book 24

**Key Myths Involving Iris:**

1. **The Trojan War**
   - Iris delivers **Zeus's commands** to the gods and mortals
   - She **rescues Hector's body** from Achilles' chariot
   - She **guides Priam** to Achilles to ransom Hector's body

2. **The Judgment of Paris**
   - Iris **attends the divine gathering** on Mount Ida
   - She **witnesses the golden apple** contest that starts the Trojan War

3. **Heracles' Apotheosis**
   - Iris **accompanies Athena** to fetch Heracles to Olympus
   - She **helps prepare** the hero for his divine transformation

## Egyptian Mythology: The Flower of Isis

### Connection to Isis

In **Ancient Egypt**, the iris was associated with **Isis**, the **goddess of magic, motherhood, and healing**. The blue iris was particularly sacred to her.

```
Isis and the Iris:
├── **Name Connection**: "Isis" may be linguistically related to "iris"
├── **Symbol**: The iris represented **rebirth and resurrection**
├── **Use**: Iris petals were used in **rituals and cosmetics**
├── **Color**: Blue irises symbolized **the heavens and the Nile**
└── **Protection**: Iris amulets were worn for **divine protection**
```

### The Iris in Egyptian Burial Practices

> **"The iris was placed in tombs to guide the soul through the afterlife, its rainbow colors representing the journey from death to rebirth."**

**Uses in Burial Rituals:**
- **Tomb Offerings**: Iris flowers were placed in tombs
- **Cosmetics**: Ground iris petals were used as **eye makeup** (kohl)
- **Perfumes**: Iris oil was used in **funerary incense**
- **Amulets**: Iris-shaped amulets **protected the deceased**

### The Book of the Dead

In the **Egyptian Book of the Dead**, the iris appears in **spells for protection and safe passage**:

```
Spell for Safe Passage:
├── "I am the iris, the flower of Isis"
├── "I open the ways of the Duat (underworld)"
├── "My colors are the spectrum of Ra"
└── "I guide the soul to the Field of Reeds"
```

## Roman Mythology: The Flower of Juno

### Juno's Flower

In **Roman mythology**, the iris was sacred to **Juno**, the **queen of the gods** and protector of women. The Romans called the iris **"Juno's flower"** or **"the sword lily"** (gladius).

```
Juno and the Iris:
├── **Protection**: Iris was planted near **temples of Juno**
├── **Marriage**: Iris flowers were used in **wedding ceremonies**
├── **Fertility**: Iris symbolized **female fertility and protection**
├── **Divination**: Iris was used in **oracles and prophecies**
└── **War**: The sword-like leaves symbolized **Juno's warrior aspect**
```

### The Iris in Roman Rituals

- **Wedding Ceremonies**: Brides carried iris flowers for **blessings of fertility**
- **Temple Offerings**: Iris flowers were left at **Juno's altars**
- **Military Rituals**: Soldiers carried iris for **protection in battle**
- **Agricultural Festivals**: Iris was used in **rites for good harvests**

## Christian Symbolism: The Trinity Flower

### The Fleur-de-lis

The **fleur-de-lis** (flower of the lily), a **stylized iris**, became one of the most important symbols in **Christian heraldry** and **royal iconography**.

```
Fleur-de-lis Symbolism:
├── **Trinity**: Three petals represent the **Father, Son, and Holy Spirit**
├── **Purity**: White iris symbolizes the **Virgin Mary**
├── **Royalty**: Associated with **French monarchy** and divine right
├── **Perfection**: Represents **divine perfection and unity**
└── **Light**: Symbolizes **divine illumination**
```

### The Madonna Lily

While often confused with true lilies, the **white iris** was associated with the **Virgin Mary** in medieval Christian art:

> **"The iris, with its sword-like leaves, represents the sorrows that would pierce Mary's heart, while its beautiful flowers symbolize her purity and grace."**

**Christian Associations:**
- **Annunciation**: Iris appears in **paintings of the Annunciation**
- **Assumption**: Iris symbolizes **Mary's assumption into heaven**
- **Saints**: Iris is associated with **Saint Joseph** and **Saint Anthony**
- **Easter**: Iris blooms were used in **Easter decorations**

## Japanese Symbolism: The Warrior's Flower

### Shōbu: The Iris Festival

In **Japan**, the iris (shōbu, 菖蒲) holds deep cultural significance, particularly for its **masculine energy** and **protective qualities**.

```
Shōbu (Japanese Iris) Symbolism:
├── **Strength**: Represents **masculine strength and courage**
├── **Purification**: Used to **ward off evil spirits**
├── **Protection**: Planted around homes for **safety**
├── **Warrior Spirit**: Associated with **samurai culture**
└── **Longevity**: Symbolizes **long life and good fortune**
```

### Tango no Sekku (Boy's Day)

The iris is central to **Tango no Sekku** (端午の節句), the **Boy's Day festival** celebrated on May 5th:

```md
**Tango no Sekku Traditions**:

1. **Iris Baths** (Shōbu-yu, 菖蒲湯):
   - Families bathe in **water infused with iris leaves**
   - Believed to **ward off disease and evil spirits**
   - Strengthens **children's health**

2. **Iris Decorations**:
   - Iris leaves are **hung on doors** for protection
   - Iris flowers are **displayed in homes**
   - **Iris-shaped mochi** (rice cakes) are eaten

3. **Samurai Connection**:
   - The **sword-like leaves** symbolize the samurai's sword
   - Iris represents the **warrior spirit**
   - Boys receive **iris-themed gifts** to encourage strength

4. **Koinobori (Carp Streamers)**:
   - Often decorated with **iris motifs**
   - Symbolize **wishes for boys' success and health**
```

├── **Good Fortune**: Brings **luck and prosperity**

### The Iris in Chinese Art and Literature

> — Chinese Poem, Tang Dynasty

**Cultural References:**
- **Poetry**: Iris appears in **classical Chinese poetry**

### Van Gogh's *Irises*

Vincent van Gogh's **1889 painting *Irises*** is one of the most famous iris depictions in art history:

```md
**About the Painting**:
- **Created**: May 1889, while Van Gogh was at the Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum
- **Medium**: Oil on canvas
- **Size**: 74 cm × 93 cm
- **Location**: J. Paul Getty Museum, Los Angeles
- **Value**: Sold for **$53.9 million** in 1987 (then a record for a painting)

**Symbolism in the Painting**:
- **Vibrant colors**: Represent Van Gogh's **emotional state**
- **Curved lines**: Symbolize the **flow of life and energy**
- **Contrast**: Between the **delicate flowers** and **bold colors**
- **Movement**: The painting seems to **pulse with life**

**Van Gogh's Words**:
> "The irises are emerging from the earth, they are taking on color. It is the most beautiful thing to see these flowers open, to watch them grow."
```
